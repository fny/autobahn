# Lane 2a notes

## F-M-SUP M-12 part B (progress frames, silence detection)
- `MuxResponse` is now an enum: `Response { channel, response }` and `Progress { channel, counter }` (the ticket's name).
- Counter: the per-channel `SideProgress` the agent already had for scan reports. Added `SideProgress::pulse()`, a `work` field and `activity()` (sum of entries, bytes, applied, work), plus a `Pulsing<T>` Read/Write/Seek adapter. **Small edit outside the region: `src/progress.rs`** (no other wave-2 lane owns it).
- Pulses: scan digest loop (scan/mod.rs); in local.rs the supply thread's deltify reader, the patch base, `base_signature`, `staged_content_matches`, `content_matches`, `copy_into_private`, and each change applied on a spread helper. `Transitioner` got a `progress` field for this (the three hashing/copy helpers take an `Option<&SideProgress>`). rsync/mod.rs itself is unchanged: its readers are wrapped at the call sites instead.
- Agent: one reporter thread per connection, every 5 s (100 ms under cfg(test)), sends Progress only for channels whose counter moved. No "request in flight" flag: a counter only moves during a request, and the controller ignores Progress for a channel that owes nothing.
- Controller: slots track an owed-request queue (replaces the `outstanding` count) and `heard`. A watchdog thread per connection (`autobahn-watchdog`, holds a Weak) checks every limit/6 (max 10 s). A change wait gets its own `milliseconds` as grace. A repeated counter, or a ScanProgress with unchanged counts (it is sent on a timer), does not count as life.
- Departure: a frame part way across the stream (either way) with bytes moving within the limit excuses every channel and restarts their clocks. Without that, one 8 MiB push/batch on a link under ~1.2 Mbit/s would read as silence.
- Peering attach mode is covered without extra code: `attach_as_agent` runs `serve_agent` (same reporter), and the leader's end is an ordinary `AgentConnection` (same watchdog).
- Not done here (part A / other lanes already did it): setup deadlines and the control-socket client timeouts (E2).
